The field guide

What is
a stinkpipe?

A tall pipe with a practical purpose: releasing sewer gases above street level.

Find one on the map

Purpose

Ventilation above the street

A stinkpipe is a sewer ventilation pipe. It connects the underground sewer to an opening high above the pavement. This lets gases disperse above the level where people would usually smell or breathe them.

The height serves a purpose. The pipe is part of the sewer system, even when its decorated column resembles other street furniture.

Source: Historic England: Victorian ‘Stink’ Pipe Listed.

London

The Great Stink and new sewers

By the middle of the nineteenth century, London’s waste was polluting the Thames. The city also suffered repeated cholera outbreaks. During the hot summer of 1858, the smell became known as the Great Stink.

The crisis helped prompt major sewer improvements. Between 1859 and 1875, engineer Joseph Bazalgette developed a network of large sewers. They intercepted sewage before it reached the central Thames and carried it east. Gravity and pumping stations moved the waste through the system.

Stinkpipes formed part of the wider effort to ventilate sewers. They released gases; they did not treat the sewage itself.

Sources: Thames Water: History of London’s sewer system; Historic England.

Survival

Small structures, local history

These pipes were not limited to London. Historic England records a surviving example in Shifnal, Shropshire. It probably followed local sewer improvements after the Public Health Act of 1875.

The Shifnal pipe received Grade II listed status in 2023. Its decorated base and survival at full height help explain its importance. Other surviving pipes have lost their upper sections.

A pipe’s appearance alone does not establish its age, maker or present use. The atlas keeps historical map reports separate from reviewed community contributions.

Source: Historic England: the Shifnal pipe.
